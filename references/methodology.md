# The Groundwork methodology

Groundwork exists to move a founder from

> "I think this is a problem."

to

> "I have credible evidence that a specific group experiences this problem
> frequently, urgently, and strongly enough to change their behaviour or pay
> for a solution."

It does that by making the *quality* of evidence visible, and by refusing to
let vague validation, compliments, hypotheticals, and leading questions
masquerade as proof.

The interview approach is grounded in **The Mom Test** (Rob Fitzpatrick),
alongside customer development, lean startup experimentation, jobs-to-be-done
interviews, and evidence-based product discovery. No book text is reproduced;
these are the underlying ideas, expressed as Groundwork's own working rules.

## The 7 principles

1. **Ask about the past, not the future.** What someone *did* is evidence.
   What they *say they would do* is a prediction, and people are bad at it.
   Interview guides lead with "tell me about the last time…"; the question
   checker flags "would you use…" as weak. See `interviewing.md`.

2. **Behaviour > opinion; commitment > enthusiasm.** Every extracted piece of
   evidence is classified by kind (past behaviour, current behaviour, existing
   commitment, new commitment, opinion, hypothetical, compliment,
   contradiction) and **weighted** accordingly. Compliments are worth zero.
   Contradictions subtract. See `scoring.md` for the exact weights.

3. **Separate the problem from your solution.** Project setup checks whether
   the problem statement is secretly a product pitch and asks the founder to
   rewrite it as a customer problem with a consequence. See `framing.md`.

4. **Decide what would prove you wrong, first.** Each hypothesis requires
   *disconfirming evidence* — a description of what observation would refute
   it — **before** any interviews happen. This is the main defence against
   confirmation bias. A hypothesis file with an empty "What would prove this
   wrong" section is not ready to test.

5. **Evidence strength is not interview count.** A hypothesis backed by five
   compliments is *weaker* than one backed by two accounts of real spend.
   Consolidation weighs quality and never concludes by majority vote. Never
   report progress as "N interviews done"; report it as evidence strength per
   hypothesis.

6. **Keep the original separate from the interpretation.** Every evidence item
   stores the verbatim quote apart from both the founder's read and the AI's
   read. Never invent, trim-to-distort, or paraphrase a quote that wasn't in
   the notes. The founder's interpretation field belongs to the founder; leave
   it blank until they fill it, and never overwrite it.

7. **Frequency ≠ urgency ≠ willingness to pay.** These are distinct
   hypotheses and must be tested separately. A problem that is clearly
   *frequent* but whose *urgency* is unproven means the next step is to go
   test consequence — not to celebrate.

## AI safety rules (apply to every task in this skill)

- Never fabricate a customer quote; quotes are verbatim from the notes.
- Never present weak evidence as fact; preserve uncertainty.
- Never treat compliments as validation or interview count as evidence
  quality.
- Always explain reasoning and point back to the source text.
- Always keep the founder's interpretation separate and easy to correct.
- The founder owns every decision; the skill only advises.

## Deliberate non-goals

Groundwork is validation reasoning, not logistics: no CRM, outreach
automation, scheduling, call recording, or billing. Don't drift into building
those; keep the founder pointed at evidence.
